## Same model, no tools, no system prompt

For a month-long rental in March (which is peak "Jacaranda season" in Mexico City), a budget of **$1,500 USD** (approx. 25,000–26,000 MXN) is very healthy. However, because March is one of the most popular months for tourists and digital nomads, the hyper-popular neighborhoods (Roma Norte and Condesa) will be highly competitive and priced at the absolute ceiling of your budget.

To get the **fastest, most reliable home internet** (which in Mexico City means fiber-optic connection, or *fibra óptica*, primarily through providers **Totalplay** or **Telmex Infinitum**), you should target neighborhoods with modern infrastructure. 

Here are the best Mexico City neighborhoods that fit your budget and offer the fastest internet speeds.

---

### 1. Narvarte (Narvarte Poniente / Oriente)
*The Best Overall Value for Remote Workers*
* **The Vibe:** Middle-class, traditional, safe, and famous for having some of the best taco stands in the city. It is quieter than Roma but highly walkable.
* **Internet Quality:** Excellent. Because it is a highly residential area populated by local professionals, the fiber-optic infrastructure (especially Totalplay) is incredibly stable. You can easily get 200–500 Mbps.
* **March Budget ($1,500 USD):** Your money goes very far here. You can easily find a modern, spacious 1- or 2-bedroom apartment with a balcony for $1,000–$1,300 USD, leaving you room to spare.

### 2. Escandón
*The "Next to Condesa" Alternative*
* **The Vibe:** Directly bordering Condesa and Roma Sur, Escandón offers a more local, relaxed neighborhood feel but puts you a 10-minute walk away from the trendy cafes and parks. 
* **Internet Quality:** High-speed fiber-optic is widely available. Since many buildings are either newly built or recently renovated, the internal wiring supports top-tier speeds.
* **March Budget ($1,500 USD):** You can find very stylish 1-bedroom apartments or lofts for $1,100–$1,400 USD. It is significantly cheaper than Condesa but shares the same geographic advantages.

### 3. Juárez & Cuauhtémoc
*For the Urban/Central Vibe*
* **The Vibe:** Located just north of Roma and bordering the Reforma business corridor. Juárez is trendy, filled with specialty coffee shops, and highly international. Cuauhtémoc is slightly quieter but very central.
* **Internet Quality:** Outstanding. Because this is adjacent to the financial district, the fiber-optic grid here is prioritized. Speeds up to 1 Gbps are available.
* **March Budget ($1,500 USD):** $1,500 is the sweet spot here. You can get a modern, well-decorated 1-bedroom apartment. 

### 4. Roma Sur
*Trendy Vibe, Better Prices*
* **The Vibe:** Roma Norte is the epicenter of tourism, but Roma Sur is more residential, less noisy, and significantly cheaper, while still being highly walkable to all the trendiest spots.
* **Internet Quality:** Excellent, but with a caveat: make sure the apartment building itself is relatively modern. Older, colonial buildings in Roma have thick adobe/brick walls that can degrade Wi-Fi signals. 
* **March Budget ($1,500 USD):** While Roma Norte is pushing $1,800–$2,500 for March, Roma Sur still has plenty of great 1-bedroom Airbnb/Flats options in the $1,200–$1,500 range.

### 5. Del Valle (Del Valle Centro / Norte)
*Safe, Green, and Highly Connected*
* **The Vibe:** A massive, leafy, upper-middle-class neighborhood. It is incredibly safe, family-friendly, and has great parks. It is less "hip" but highly functional.
* **Internet Quality:** Among the best in the city. Totalplay (the fastest provider) has massive coverage here.
* **March Budget ($1,500 USD):** You can easily rent a luxury 1-bedroom or a very large 2-bedroom apartment with high-end amenities for $1,100–$1,400 USD.

### How to Guarantee Fast Internet Before Booking
In Mexico City, internet speed depends more on the **provider and contract** than the neighborhood itself. Even in the best neighborhood, a host might buy the cheapest package. 

Before booking an Airbnb, Vrbo, or monthly rental (via platforms like Midasa or Homie), send this template to the host:

> *"Hola, trabajo desde casa y necesito internet muy rápido y estable. ¿Qué proveedor de internet tienes (Totalplay, Telmex, Izzi)? ¿Es fibra óptica? ¿Podrías hacerme el favor de hacer una prueba en Speedtest.net y mandarme una captura de pantalla de la velocidad de bajada y subida? ¡Muchas gracias!"*

**What to look for in their reply:**
*   **Provider:** **Totalplay** is currently the most reliable and fastest in CDMX, followed closely by **Telmex Infinitum (Fibra)**. Avoid **Izzi** if possible, as their upload speeds are often capped very low.
*   **Speeds:** Look for at least **100+ Mbps download** and **30+ Mbps upload** on the screenshot.

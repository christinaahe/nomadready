With a budget of **$1,500 USD per month** (approximately $25,000–$26,000 MXN), you have excellent options in Mexico City. While March is peak "snowbird" and digital nomad season (meaning Condesa and Roma Norte will be at their most expensive and competitive), you can easily find high-speed fiber-optic internet and great apartments just a few blocks outside of these hyper-gentrified zones.

Here are the best neighborhoods in CDMX that fit your criteria, along with details on how to secure the fastest internet.

---

### 1. Narvarte (Poniente & Oriente)
*The Best Overall Value & Local Vibe*
Narvarte is the premier neighborhood for remote workers looking for a "real" Mexico City feel without paying Condesa prices. It is incredibly safe, flat, highly walkable, and famous for having some of the best tacos in the city.
*   **Internet Quality:** Excellent. The neighborhood has modern infrastructure. Fiber optic providers like **Totalplay** and **Telmex Infinitum** are standard here. You can easily get **200 to 500 Mbps** symmetrical speeds.
*   **March Budget ($1,500 USD):** You can easily get a modern, spacious 1- or 2-bedroom apartment here for $1,000–$1,300 USD, leaving you room in your budget.

### 2. Escandón (I & II)
*The "Condesa-Adjacent" Budget Savior*
Located directly west of Condesa (just across the Patriotismo avenue), Escandón offers the same leafy streets and Art Deco architecture but at a 30-40% discount. It is highly walkabe and shares the same infrastructure as its wealthier neighbors.
*   **Internet Quality:** Superb. Because it borders Condesa and San Miguel Chapultepec, the fiber-optic grid here is highly prioritized and modern.
*   **March Budget ($1,500 USD):** You can find stylish 1-bedroom lofts or apartments for $1,100–$1,400 USD, even in peak March. 

### 3. Roma Sur
*The Cooler, Cheaper Sibling of Roma Norte*
Roma Norte is likely out of budget for a decent place in March, but Roma Sur (south of Coahuila street) is highly feasible. It is slightly grittier and more residential but has the same bohemian charm, great cafes, and immediate access to the Metro/Metrobus.
*   **Internet Quality:** Outstanding. Being part of the "Roma-Condesa" corridor, ISPs have installed their fastest fiber lines here.
*   **March Budget ($1,500 USD):** Nice 1-bedroom apartments and studios hover right around $1,200–$1,500 USD on Airbnb with monthly discounts.

### 4. Cuauhtémoc / San Rafael
*Central, Urban, and High-Speed*
Cuauhtémoc is located just north of the Reforma financial district. It is highly urban, bustling, and filled with expats, while San Rafael (just north of it) is a trendy, up-and-coming arts district. 
*   **Internet Quality:** Because Cuauhtémoc borders the business district, the internet infrastructure is some of the fastest in the country. Almost all buildings have access to **Totalplay Fiber**.
*   **March Budget ($1,500 USD):** In Cuauhtémoc, a sleek studio will run around $1,300–$1,500 USD. In San Rafael, you can get a large 1- or 2-bedroom for $1,000–$1,200 USD.

### 5. Del Valle (Norte / Centro)
*Safe, Green, and Highly Connected*
Del Valle is a wealthy, safe, family-oriented neighborhood south of Narvarte. It is incredibly quiet and green, with great parks. It isn't as "hip" as Roma, but it is highly efficient and safe.
*   **Internet Quality:** Excellent. It is a highly affluent neighborhood, so high-speed fiber optic connections (up to 1 Gbps) are standard in almost all buildings.
*   **March Budget ($1,500 USD):** You can get a very nice, modern 1- or 2-bedroom apartment here for $1,000–$1,300 USD.

### How to Guarantee Fast Internet in CDMX

In Mexico City, the *neighborhood* doesn’t guarantee speed; the **provider** and **modem setup** do. 

1.  **Ask for the Provider:** Before booking, message the host and ask which company they use. 
    *   **Totalplay (Fiber/Fibra Óptica):** This is the gold standard in CDMX. It is incredibly fast and rarely goes down.
    *   **Telmex Infinitum (Fiber):** Also excellent, provided it is the *fiber* version and not the old copper DSL version.
    *   **Izzi:** Generally okay, but traditionally uses coaxial cable, which has slower upload speeds and is more prone to outages during heavy rains.
2.  **Demand a Speed Test:** Never book a place without asking the host to run a speed test (via speedtest.net) and send you a screenshot. If they are hesitant, move on. For Zoom calls and heavy uploads, look for at least **100 Mbps download / 50 Mbps upload**.
3.  **Power Outages:** While rare in March (which is the dry season), power fluctuations can happen. Ask if the apartment building has a backup generator, or bring a small mini-UPS (uninterruptible power supply) for the router if your work is critical.

### Where to Book for March
Since March is peak season, booking via **Airbnb** or **Vrbo** can be expensive due to platform fees. To stay under $1,500:
*   **Look for Monthly Discounts:** Airbnb hosts often offer 30% to 50% discounts for stays of 28+ days.
*   **Mid-Term Rental Sites:** Check out platforms like **Flatio** or **Homie.mx** (though Homie may require a Mexican guarantor, some landlords bypass this for foreigners paying upfront).
*   **Facebook Groups:** Search groups like "Foreigners & Expats in Mexico City" or "Condesa/Roma Apartments sublets." You can often find direct leases from landlords for $1,000–$1,200 USD, though you must be careful of wire scams (never pay before seeing the place or having a trusted contact video-call you from the apartment).

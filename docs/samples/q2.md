### 1. Narvarte (Poniente & Oriente) – *The Digital Nomad Sweet Spot*
Narvarte is widely considered the best neighborhood for remote workers who want a local feel, great food (it’s the taco capital of CDMX), and high-speed internet without paying the "gringo tax" of Roma or Condesa.
*   **Internet:** Excellent. The neighborhood has modern infrastructure with fiber-optic cables running to almost every building. You can easily find speeds of 300+ Mbps.
*   **Budget ($1,500):** You can easily get a modern, spacious 1 or 2-bedroom apartment here for $1,000–$1,300 USD, leaving you plenty of buffer room.
*   **Vibe:** Residential, safe, filled with cafes, and highly walkable.

### 2. Roma Sur & Escandón – *Trendy Adjacent*
If you want to be close to the trendy restaurants and bars of Roma Norte and Condesa but can't afford their high March rental prices, these two bordering neighborhoods are your best bet.
*   **Internet:** Fantastic. Because they border the tech/creative hubs, ISPs have prioritized fiber-optic rollouts here. Totalplay is highly reliable in both areas.
*   **Budget ($1,500):** While Roma Norte is pushing $1,800+ for a decent 1-bedroom in March, Roma Sur and Escandón still have plenty of stylish, well-lit loft apartments for $1,200–$1,400 USD.
*   **Vibe:** A mix of old-school Mexico City charm and hip new coffee shops.

### 3. Del Valle (Centro/Norte) – *Safe, Modern & Connected*
Del Valle is an upscale, leafy residential neighborhood just south of Roma. It is incredibly safe and home to many Mexican professionals.
*   **Internet:** Some of the most stable in the city. Since it is a wealthier, highly developed district, telecom companies offer their top-tier fiber packages here. 
*   **Budget ($1,500):** You can easily find a high-end 1-bedroom apartment in a modern building with amenities (like a gym or rooftop) for $1,100–$1,400 USD.
*   **Vibe:** Quiet, green, clean, and very safe, though less "hipster" than Roma/Condesa.

### 4. San Rafael & Santa María la Ribera – *Artistic & Affordable*
Located just north of the city center (Reforma), these are historic, up-and-coming neighborhoods popular with artists and local remote workers.
*   **Internet:** Good fiber-optic coverage, though you must double-check with the host, as some older buildings may still use older copper wiring.
*   **Budget ($1,500):** Your budget goes incredibly far here. You can get a beautiful, historically preserved, or newly renovated apartment for $800–$1,100 USD.
*   **Vibe:** Bohemian, architectural, historic, and very authentic.

### Crucial Tips for Securing Fast Internet in CDMX

In Mexico City, "high-speed internet" listed on Airbnb can mean anything from a sluggish 10 Mbps DSL line to a 500 Mbps fiber-optic line. Do not leave this to chance.

1.  **Ask the Host for a Speedtest Screenshot:** Before booking, message the host and ask: *"¿Me podrías compartir una captura de pantalla de un test de velocidad (Speedtest.net) del internet de la casa?"* (Could you share a screenshot of a speed test of the home internet?).
2.  **Ask for the Provider:** **Totalplay** is currently the gold standard for speed and reliability in CDMX. **Telmex (Infinitum)** is also excellent *if* it is fiber-optic (ask if it is "Fibra Óptica"). Avoid providers like Izzi if you need high upload speeds for video calls.
3.  **Use "Monthly" Discounts on Airbnb:** If booking via Airbnb, search for exactly 28+ days. Many hosts offer a 30% to 50% discount for month-long stays, which will bring high-end $2,000/month apartments down into your $1,500 range.
4.  **Have a Backup:** Power outages or construction can occasionally cut fiber lines. Buy a local eSIM (like Telcel or Bait) to use as a hotspot in case of emergencies. Telcel has excellent 5G coverage in all the neighborhoods listed above.
